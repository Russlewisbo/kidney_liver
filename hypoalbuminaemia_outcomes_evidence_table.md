# Hypoalbuminaemia and clinical outcomes with highly protein-bound anti-infectives — evidence table

Compiled 17 Nov 2026 from primary abstracts/full texts (Undermind semantic search + Acbo 2025 focused review, doi 10.1007/s40495-025-00397-z). All clinical studies are observational; none measured unbound drug concentrations. Hypoalbuminaemia = albumin < 2.5 g/dL unless stated.

## A. Clinical outcome studies

| Drug (binding) | Study | Design, n | Population | Comparison | Outcome | Result | What it can and cannot show |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ertapenem (85–95%) vs imipenem/meropenem (2–20%) | Zusman 2015, Clin Microbiol Infect 21:54 | Prospective cohort, n = 279 (173 ertapenem, 106 I/M) | Microbiologically documented Enterobacteriaceae infection | Hypoalbuminaemia vs normal, within each drug group | 30-day mortality | OR 4.6 (2.1–10.1) with ertapenem vs OR 1.2 (0.5–2.7) with I/M; P = 0.02 for difference. In I/M group mortality 35% vs 29% | The only study with a low-binding internal control; the drug-by-albumin interaction is what separates a drug effect from albumin as a severity marker. | Teicoplanin (90%) | Roberts 2014 (DALI), IJAA 43:423 | Prospective PK, n = 13 | ICU | Albumin vs unbound fraction | PK only | Unbound fraction highly variable, higher at low albumin; Yano formula mispredicts free teicoplanin | No outcome data |

## B. Pharmacological frame

| Source | Point |
|---|---|
| Benet & Hoener 2002, Clin Pharmacol Ther 71:115 | For low-extraction drugs, steady-state unbound exposure = dose / intrinsic clearance; a fall in albumin lowers total, not free, average concentration |
| Gandia 2023, Antibiotics 12:515 | Literature review 1975–2021: < 11% of articles on hypoalbuminaemia and PK contained no interpretive error; hypoalbuminaemia does not change pharmacologically active exposure except for high-extraction, highly bound drugs |
| T'Jollyn 2018, Clin Pharmacokinet 57:59 | PBPK simulation of four drugs: lower albumin raised unbound fraction without lowering unbound exposure |
| Heffernan 2022, AAC 66:e02189-21 | Measured unbound ceftriaxone, ICU: albumin ≤ 20 g/L lowered PTA by up to 20% with intermittent dosing — the exception arises from troughs (time-dependent killing), not from average exposure |
| Wong 2013, AAC 57:6165 | Unbound concentrations predicted from published binding biased +83% (ceftriaxone), +57% (flucloxacillin) in ICU patients |
| IDSA 2024 AMR Gram-negative guidance (Tamma, CID) | Prefers meropenem/imipenem over ertapenem in critically ill or hypoalbuminaemic patients, citing Zusman 2015 and PK studies |

## C. Reading

1. Every outcome signal is observational and every cohort compared sick with sicker: albumin is one of the strongest prognostic markers in hospitalised patients, independent of any drug.
2. Only Zusman 2015 built in the control that matters — the same association tested in patients on a low-binding drug — and there it vanished (OR 1.2). That is the strongest single piece of evidence that the ertapenem signal is pharmacological. It has never been replicated.
3. The MSSA data (Shah 2025, 2026) show the same association with cefazolin, which is less bound and given at high frequency; the Moser 2021 free-drug data show deaths with high, not low, unbound flucloxacillin. Together they argue that for most of these drugs albumin is a marker, not a mechanism.
4. The pharmacology predicts exactly this pattern: unbound average exposure is preserved; what hypoalbuminaemia changes is the shape of the curve (lower troughs, larger volume) — relevant for once-daily, time-dependent drugs like ertapenem and ceftriaxone given 1 g daily, and much less for q6h cefazolin or continuous infusions. Ackerman 2020 (2 g rescues the hypoalbuminaemic subgroup) and Heffernan 2022 (continuous infusion restores PTA) are the practical corollary.
5. No outcome study measured unbound concentrations. The next study to run is simple: unbound ertapenem or ceftriaxone troughs, stratified by albumin, with outcomes.
